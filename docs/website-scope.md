# What motoproponent.com should and shouldn't be

Companion to the channel growth plan. That document is about the goal—1,000 subscribers by retirement day. This one is about the website's part in it, written before we build the real thing so that we are both agreeing to the same idea rather than discovering it halfway through.

The short version: **the channel is the product, the website is the address.** Almost every bad decision available to us comes from forgetting that.

## The three jobs it actually has

**One, an address that never changes.** Platforms rename things, suspend accounts, alter handles and retire features. `motoproponent.com` is one string you can put in a bio, an email signature, a comment reply, a sticker on the top box, or say out loud in a video, and it will still work in 2031. That is worth more than it sounds.

This is sharper in your case than usual, because `@motoproponent` was already taken on Instagram. On the domain, you are Motoproponent with no substitutions and nobody to share it with.

**Two, a one-way door to the channel.** Someone who lands there should have exactly one obvious thing to do, and that thing is watch a video. Not "explore." One door.

**Three, a place to see whether any of this works.** Because the trip goes Instagram → site → channel, we can see how many people actually make it. If you link the channel directly from your bio you get the click and learn nothing about where it came from.

## What it should be

- **One page.** It can grow later. It does not need to start big.
- **Fast on a phone with two bars.** Most visitors arrive from a phone, often outdoors, often impatient.
- **Honest about its size.** A small site that is clearly one guy is more appealing than one pretending to be a media company. The audience for motorcycle content has a finely tuned sense for this.
- **Recognisably yours.** Your face, your bikes, your gear—which you have already said is fine, and which matters because your videos are all of you.
- **Self-updating if it shows anything time-sensitive.** If the page lists recent videos, it pulls them automatically. Anything that requires you to remember to update it will eventually be wrong.

## What it shouldn't be

- **Not a blog.** Written posts are a second content job on top of the videos, and the videos are the thing being measured. A blog with three posts and a two-year gap is worse than no blog.
- **Not a competitor to your own channel.** No hosting your own video, no rebuilding a video library on the site. Every minute watched should be watched on YouTube, where it counts toward the watch-hour requirement.
- **Not a brochure.** No "our mission," no stock photography, no paragraphs of biography nobody reads. If you would not say it out loud in a video, it should not be on the page.
- **Not a store.** Merch before an audience is a reliable way to spend money and time on boxes of unsold shirts. This becomes an interesting question somewhere north of a few thousand subscribers, not now.
- **Not a newsletter signup, yet.** An email list is the one asset even more durable than the domain, and worth revisiting once there is an audience to ask. Asking 40 people to subscribe to a newsletter you then have to write is a trap.
- **Not blocked on the logo.** The ambigram is a separate piece of work with its own timeline. The site should ship and improve without waiting on it, and it should look deliberate with or without the finished mark.

## Roughly how it should grow

**Now—holding page.** Live today: the name and a single line of text. The link to the channel is built and lands with the next update. This is the correct amount of website for this stage, and it satisfies job one on its own.

**Next—the real one-pager.** Who you are in two sentences, your recent videos pulled in automatically, a clear subscribe action, and a contact address. This is the version worth building properly, and it is probably where the site stays for a long time.

**Later—when there is something to monetize.** Sponsor and collaboration enquiries, the facts a brand asks for before it emails you, and a merch link if merch ever exists. Not before someone actually asks.

## The two failure modes worth naming

**A stale site is worse than no site.** A visitor who sees "latest video" from eighteen months ago concludes the whole thing is abandoned, including the channel. Every feature we add is a promise to keep feeding it, so the fewer promises the better.

**Effort spent on the site is effort not spent filming.** The site will never be the reason someone subscribes. If a week of website work costs a video, the website lost that trade.

## What we need from you before building the real one

1. **Recent videos on the page—yes or no?** It keeps the page alive automatically, which is the main argument for it. It also gives people something to click that is not the subscribe link.
2. **Is `motoproponent@gmail.com` the address that goes on it**, or do you want something at the domain for anything sponsor-facing later?
3. **A photo we can use.** A decent still of you with a bike beats anything we would design.
4. **Two sentences in your own words.** Your channel description is already close, so this may be done.
